The people we ask to stand with us

Eight ambassador personas: who they are, who follows them, why they would back HoL, and where we find them.

House of Longevity. Ambassador program, audience book, October 2026.

Audiences first, names second

The program works through people whose audiences already look after themselves and can afford to. Alex mapped eight kinds of people. This book gives each one a face.

Four go first, in a pilot. Each one tests a different audience, so by month three we know which kind of ambassador actually brings enquiries. Three wait for phase two. One is on hold until counsel clears the category.

We chose the stages on four tests: reach to people who already buy premium services, regulatory risk, content fit, and the job each one does in the channel mix. At least two of the pilot four are women.

Every ambassador is paid in equity, discloses the partnership in every post, and never talks about their own health or care, or anyone else's. The full rules sit in the program deck.

1 of 8 Pilot, the anchor

The retired pro athlete

A former professional athlete from the NRL, AFL, rugby, cricket or the Olympics, now working in media, commentary or business.

A broad, mostly male following: tradies, FIFO workers, suburban dads. People who grew up watching them play and still trust their word.

They carry recognition into rooms the rest of the program can't reach, and they are a natural fit for events and for a guest spot on Pep In Your Step.

Start with a podcast conversation about their career, not their health. Then the partnership, paid in equity.

Where we find them
Commentary panels, club ambassador rosters and athlete management agencies.
Their own series
After the siren
The question it answers
Does broad recognition bring enquiries, or only reach?
Watch for
Existing sport and media contracts, and past sponsor conflicts.

2 of 8 Pilot

The elite performance coach

A high-end personal trainer, strength and conditioning coach or gym owner who trains athletes, executives or talent.

People already paying for premium training. For them, spending on their health is a habit, not a stretch. It is the clearest sign of premium intent we can see.

Coaches see the point where training alone stops being enough. Their word carries weight with people who already take their bodies seriously.

Equity, and a clinic that respects their craft. They post their coaching, their gym and their ideas. They never refer clients and are never paid per client.

Where we find them
Premium studios in Bondi, Surry Hills, Toorak, Teneriffe and Cottesloe, and the coaches behind professional clubs.
Their own series
Session notes
The question it answers
Do followers who already pay for premium training enquire?
Watch for
Never refers clients, never paid per client.

3 of 8 Pilot

The midlife women's strength voice

A woman in midlife who talks openly about strength training, career and family.

Professional women who invest in their strength and their work, and who are often the person deciding on health for the whole household.

This is a core premium audience. Women here want real answers and decisions in their own hands, which is what a doctor-led clinic offers.

Lead with the doctors who lead HoL. She posts about strength, work, family and community, never about symptoms.

Where we find them
Women's strength creators, presenters and community leaders.
Their own series
Barbell and board pack
The question it answers
Does a core premium audience trust the partnership?
Watch for
Talks about strength and career, never perimenopause or any symptom.

4 of 8 Pilot

The founder-operator

A founder, CEO or investor with a public voice on LinkedIn, podcasts or X, talking about leadership and the long game.

Senior professionals with little time and an invest-in-the-asset mindset. The people closest to buying premium services, and to the corporate programs that come later.

The most credible voice for the premium tier, and a door into corporate health programs.

Co-builder framing: advisory status, equity and a place in the founding story. They post about building things, leadership and why standards matter.

Where we find them
The Australian startup and VC community, and business podcasts.
Their own series
Hard Tuesday
The question it answers
Does a business audience enquire, and does it open corporate doors?
Watch for
Business conflicts. Never speaks about their own care.

5 of 8 Phase two, if the pilot works

The FIFO and resources voice

A creator from mining, trades or FIFO work: a site worker, trainer or ex-military.

Workers who are well paid and short on time while on swing. Telehealth suits their rosters. A familiar face from site beats a celebrity.

HoL already advertises on the mine network: 166 screens across 60 sites, reaching 53,995 workers in five states. This voice tests whether a person adds anything the screens don't.

Built for how you actually live and work. They post life on swing and training on roster. They never appear on the screens, and their results are tracked apart from them.

Where we find them
Mining and trades creators on TikTok and Instagram, site communities and FIFO groups.
Their own series
Swing diary
The question it answers
Does a familiar voice add enquiries beyond the screens?
Watch for
Site and employer rules. Kept separate from the screen advertising.

6 of 8 Phase two, if the pilot works

The endurance amateur

An Ironman, Hyrox, marathon or cycling creator who lives by the training block.

People with expensive hobbies who already spend on gear, coaching and race entry. Their followers plan seasons, not weekends.

Testing, tracking and planning is how this world already thinks. A doctor-led clinic sits naturally next to the coach and the bike fitter.

Training-block and race-day storytelling, with HoL as the partner in the background. Training and races only, no data about their own body.

Where we find them
Strava clubs, Hyrox gyms, triathlon podcasts and cycling YouTube.
Their own series
Bunch rules
The question it answers
Can a data-heavy culture stay off results and numbers?
Watch for
The pull toward results and body numbers.

7 of 8 Phase two, one event first

The premium lifestyle curator

A wellness, travel or hospitality creator, or the owner of a retreat or venue in Byron, Noosa or Sydney's east.

People who already spend on retreats, saunas and good places to be. They come with the venue as much as the voice.

They place HoL inside a considered life: long tables, early mornings, good places. It matches the brand's quiet tempo.

One sponsored event before any grant: a long table or morning swim they host, with HoL as a disclosed sponsor.

Where we find them
Retreat and venue owners, luxury travel creators and hospitality groups.
Their own series
Long table
The question it answers
Does one sponsored event bring enquiries before any grant?
Watch for
Must pass the test: HoL is a clinic, not a hotel or a spa.

8 of 8 On hold, until counsel clears it

The longevity optimiser

A creator covering sleep, wearables, cold and heat, and recovery.

Early adopters who are fluent in the language of longevity. Their audience overlaps most with people already buying unapproved products online.

In Alex's first draft this was the bridge from the grey market to doctor-supervised care, and it is still the market thesis. It is also the highest regulatory risk in the program.

Not approached until counsel clears the category. If cleared, screen out anyone who has pushed grey-market products or extreme claims.

Where we find them
Longevity podcasts, wearable and recovery creators, biohacking communities.
The question it answers
Held. The question comes after counsel's view.
Watch for
The highest regulatory risk. Held until counsel clears it.

Next, the names

For each pilot persona, the program owner builds a shortlist of real people. Each person is ranked on where their audience lives, signs of premium spend, their past content and their fit with HoL, using data they share at vetting.

Audience size, income and age are researched person by person, not assumed. Nobody is approached until counsel has given a written view on the program.
